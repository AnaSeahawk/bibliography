# Cabala mineralis — Rabbi Simeon ben Cantara

**Files:**
- `BenCantara-Cabala-Mineralis.pdf` — Warburg Institute scan (FGH 6480, Innes Collection); from https://wdl.warburg.sas.ac.uk/object-wdl-wco-aaez
- `BenCantara-Cabala-Mineralis-BL-Book1.pdf` — the circulating digital booklet of the **British Library copy** (Add MS 5245), each First Book plate with an English translation printed beneath (the translations match Adam McLean's, alchemywebsite.com/cab_min1.html); from archive.org item `cabala-mineralis-manuscript-book-1`
- `BenCantara-Cabala-Mineralis-BL-Book2-Plates.pdf` — the five Second Book plates of the BL copy, assembled from the images at alchemywebsite.com/cab_min2.html

**Companion contents:** full Latin transcription of every caption and label in the Warburg manuscript with English translation; the BL copy's Second Book plates; and the complete text of "The practise of Mary the Prophetess in the Alchymicall Art" (the Second Book's text).

## How this was made, and what to trust

The transcription below is my own reading of the 17th-century hand from the Warburg scans (PDF pages 7–17; the rest of the file is covers, endpapers, and blanks). The hand is unusually clean and most readings are secure. Where a reading is uncertain it is marked `[?]`; where the scribe's spelling is non-classical I have kept it as written and noted the standard form in brackets. The translations are working translations, not scholarly ones — check them against the Latin, which is short enough to read line by line.

The manuscript uses astrological sigils, rendered here as: ☉ = Sol (gold), ☽ = Luna (silver). The scribe adds case endings as superscripts (☉is = Solis, ☽ae = Lunae).

**A note on this copy:** the better-known copy of the *Cabala mineralis* is British Library Add MS 5245, which carries captions throughout both parts. In this Warburg copy the second part is a title spread and figure drawings only — the ruled caption boxes and a speech-scroll are left empty, and several caption boxes in part one are also blank. It looks like an unfinished copy, but that is my inference from the blanks, not something the catalogue record states.

## Plate 1 — the mine of our Mercury (PDF p. 8)

*Scene:* under a tree, a boy in red stoops to the ground holding tongs, an hourglass on his head labelled **Mercury**; the ground is labelled **Fodina** (the mine). A flowering plant with blue blossoms stands in a pot over a small fire, eagles perched on it, labelled **ponticitas**. A second figure wrings a cloth (label small and blurred: `Volatile [?]`) over a shallow pan held by a disembodied hand; below, a flask on a fire surrounded by a flock of eagles, and at far right a small sealed vessel over fire.

**Latin (caption box):**

> Fodina Mercurii nostri sal petra nostrum non vulgi.
> Ponticitas vitriolum nostrum non vulgi Acuatio.
> Armoniacum nostrum non vulgi.

**English:**

> The mine of our Mercury; our saltpetre, not the common sort.
> Ponticity [sharpness]: our vitriol, not the common — the sharpening.
> Our [sal] ammoniac, not the common.

The point of the triple "non vulgi" is that the named substances (saltpetre, vitriol, sal ammoniac) are cover-names: the work does not use the apothecary's materials but "ours," drawn from the *fodina*.

## Plate 2 — the seed of Mercury (PDF p. 10)

*Scene, left:* a green winged dragon lies on a dish under raining clouds; labels **Mercurii Germen** and, beneath the dish, **Hermaphroditum**. *Centre:* a cucurbit with still-head over fire, and inside it a child, shown urinating, labelled **Mercurius Homogeneus**. *Right:* a long-necked flask over fire; inside, a caduceus rises from a yellow deposit, labelled **Mercurius vivus**.

**Latin (left caption box):**

> Mercurii Germen, Aquae vivae, volatilis, partes, novem, Fixae pars una Mercurii, Homogenitas Aqua viva Acuata.

**English:**

> The Seed of Mercury: nine parts of the volatile living water, one part of the fixed [part] of Mercury. Homogeneity: the living water sharpened.

**Latin (right caption box):**

> Mercurius noster Aqua viva pontica.

**English:**

> Our Mercury: the pontic [sharp] living water.

This is the plate behind the common reading (argued by Adam McLean among others) that the manuscript's raw matter is urine: the child urinating inside the vessel labelled "homogeneous Mercury" is hard to read any other way, though the text itself never says the word — it speaks only of *aqua viva*.

## Plate 3 — amalgamation and philosophic calcination (PDF p. 11)

*Scene:* two flasks over fire, each holding a winged cherub standing behind a dark spongy mass, eagles flying at both sides. In the left flask the cherub holds a crescent moon; in the right, a sun, with labels **Calcinatio Sophica** and **Solis** around the flask.

**Latin (left caption box):**

> Duae, ad plus Tres partes, Mercurii nostri, Liguant [= ligant] unam partem ☽ae vel ☉is, vulgi subtilitati, et Corpus unum, fiunt, Spongiosum, inseparabile, Quod vocatur ☽ nostra, vel ☉ noster, non vulgi
> ☽. per Mercurium Aquilarum Quinque
> ☉. per Mercurium Aquilarum Septem.

**English:**

> Two, at most three parts of our Mercury bind one part of common Silver or Gold [first] subtilised, and they become one body, spongy, inseparable, which is called our Silver or our Gold, not the common.
> Silver: by the Mercury of five eagles.
> Gold: by the Mercury of seven eagles.

("Eagles" is the standard cover-term for repeated sublimations — the eagle-flocks drawn around the vessels throughout the manuscript are counting sublimations, not decorating.)

The right caption box is ruled but empty.

## Plate 4 — putrefaction and germination (PDF p. 12)

*Scene, left:* flask over fire, contents jet black, cherub standing in it, three eagles; labels **putrefactio Sophica**. *Right:* flask over fire whose dark surface shows red glints and rising smoke; labels **Germinatio** and **Vegetatica** [? possibly *Vegetativa*]. A ribbon-scroll connects the two flasks.

**Latin (left caption box):**

> ☉. noster, de Calcinatione, nostra, irrigatam [sic; sense requires *irrigatus*] novi Mercurii, partibus duabus in pondere, ☉is vulgi fit corpus unum nigerrimum non Spongiosum.

**English:**

> Our Gold, from our calcination, irrigated with new Mercury — two parts by weight against the common gold — becomes one utterly black body, not spongy.

**Latin (right caption box):**

> ☉ de nigredine Colore Mutatô viridascens Germen, suum pandit in vegetationem.

**English:**

> The Gold, its colour changed from blackness, growing green, unfolds its seed into vegetation.

## Plate 5 — the White Sulphur and the wet volatilisation (PDF p. 13)

*Scene, left:* flask over fire half-filled with white scaly grains, labelled **Sulphur Album**. *Right:* flask over fire, dark iridescent surface with coloured vapours above, labels **Liquidum** and (vertically) **volatilisatio per**. Ribbon-scroll between them.

**Latin (left caption box):**

> ☉ noster mutatô Colore virid[i] [?], Transit in Sulphur Album, Quod est Corpus Animatum Animâ inCorruptibili.

**English:**

> Our Gold, its green colour changed, passes into the White Sulphur, which is a body animated by an incorruptible soul.

**Latin (right caption box):**

> ☉. Hic noster albus, sine Manuum impositione ulla, nec vasis, Apertione. iterum liquescit, Calore Continuatô cum variis vaporibus. ad. cauda pavonis. similitudinem, Tunc ☉ noster. Albus. volatilisatur per Liquidum.

**English:**

> This our white Gold — without any laying-on of hands, and without opening the vessel — liquefies again under continued heat, with various vapours in the likeness of the peacock's tail; then our white Gold is volatilised through the liquid [the wet way].

Note the operative claim: from here the work proceeds *sine manuum impositione* — sealed, untouched, driven by heat alone.

---

## Plate 6 — the dry volatilisation and the Red Sulphur (PDF p. 14)

*Scene, left:* flask over fire, contents drying from brown to red, labels (vertically) **volatilisatio per** and **siccum**. *Right:* flask over fire filled with deep-red grains, labelled **Sulphur Rubeum**. Ribbon-scroll between them.

**Latin (left caption box):**

> Continuatô Calore ☉ noster volatilis, jam factus per Liquidum desiccatur et fit Corpus Lucens, Quasi Aurora Consurgens, Haec est volatilisatio per siccum.

**English:**

> With continued heat our volatile Gold, now made [volatile] through the liquid, is dried and becomes a shining body, like the rising dawn. This is volatilisation by the dry [way].

**Latin (right caption box):**

> Hac Aurora paulatim Evanescente, consurgit ☉ noster in Granula pulchrima [= pulcherrima], et Rubicundissima, Que [= Quae] sunt Sulphur nostrum Rubeum a Sophis, Tam desideratum, Quod tamen non est finis Laborum.

**English:**

> As this dawn gradually fades, our Gold rises up into little grains, most beautiful and deepest red, which are our Red Sulphur so longed for by the sages — which nevertheless is not the end of the labours.

## Plate 7 — imbibition and the Stone (PDF p. 15)

*Scene, left:* flask over fire; inside, a cherub stands in a landscape of coloured grains with smoke and small flames, labels around the flask: **fermentatio, alias Imbibitio Lapidis**. *Right:* flask (no fire beneath) half-filled with red grains, labelled **Lapis Sophorum**, and beneath it **Medecina tertii ordinis**. Ribbon-scroll between them.

**Latin (left caption box):**

> Sulphur hoc rubeum, imbibitur novi Mercurii Quinario, sui corporis pondere, paulatim et paulatim, per septem diversas Imbibitiones, et continuatô Calore Rotatâ rotâ Naturae, mense unô, Transeunt putrefactio et jam dicta Regimina.

**English:**

> This red Sulphur is imbibed with a fivefold [measure] of new Mercury, by the weight of its own body, little by little, through seven distinct imbibitions; and with continued heat, the wheel of Nature having been turned, in one month the putrefaction and the regimens already described pass by [again].

**Latin (right caption box):**

> Finito Mense, surgit Rex, omnipotens, seu Lapis noster, perfecta Medecina, tertii ordinis, potens in sui projectione, omnia Metalla transmutare.
> Deo. sit Aeterna Laus et Gratiarum actio incessabilis Amen.

**English:**

> The month being finished, there arises the almighty King, or our Stone, the perfect Medicine of the third order, powerful in its projection to transmute all metals.
> To God be eternal praise and unceasing thanksgiving. Amen.

## The Second part of this Booke (PDF pp. 16–17)

The part-title (PDF p. 16) is in **English**, not Latin — the only English in the manuscript:

> The Second part of this Booke.

*Scene:* a full-spread landscape. An old bearded man (Saturn-type) leaps barefoot above the ground, holding a pair of compasses in one hand and a branch of red berries in the other, a latticed sphere hanging below; above him a face-in-sun sheds gold coin-like stars. To the right: a white dog chasing a hare up a hill, a palm tree, a red ox, and a black swan sitting on a nest.

PDF p. 17 carries four figure drawings (a man lying on a rolled mat with drops falling from his body and an empty speech-scroll at his mouth; an old man reaching upward; a flying winged figure in blue; a standing youth) — **no text**; the scroll and the following ruled spreads are blank.

# The British Library copy (Add MS 5245) and the Second Book

The comparison the Warburg copy called for. The BL copy's First Book runs the same seven-plate sequence with the same Latin captions (`BL-Book1.pdf` prints McLean's English under each plate — useful as a check against my translations above; they agree in substance throughout).

## Second Book plates (`BL-Book2-Plates.pdf`)

The BL Second Book is richer than the Warburg one — five plates against the Warburg copy's two, and still without captions in either copy:

1. "The Second part of this Booke" — the leaping greybeard with compasses and berry-branch, latticed sphere, coin-shedding sun; dog chasing hare, palm, red ox, black swan on nest. (Same scene as Warburg PDF p. 16.)
2. Two roundels: fire under a dark field with white seed-pearls at the horizon of rain-clouds and sun; below, fire fuming under a cloud-band. **Not in the Warburg copy.**
3. Two roundels under sun-moon-stars: a fissured black landscape with fire; below, a child in the fissured field held by a hand from a cloud. **Not in the Warburg copy.**
4. The man lying on a rolled mat, drops falling from his body, scroll at his mouth; below, the old man reaching upward. (Warburg PDF p. 17, where the scroll is blank too.)
5. The flying winged figure in blue above the standing youth. (Warburg PDF p. 17.)

## The Second Book's text: The practise of Mary the Prophetess in the Alchymicall Art

The picture-only Second Book travels with this text in the circulating editions. It is the *Dialogue of Mary and Aros*, the central Latin-tradition testimony of Mary the Jewess (compare Patai, *The Jewish Alchemists*). The transcription below is from BL **MS Sloane 3641** fols. 1–8 (as given at alchemywebsite.com/maryprof.html); the text was printed in *Auriferae artis* (1572), *Alchymia vera* (1604), Arnaldus de Villa Nova *Opus Aureum* (1604), *Lumen chymicum novum* (1624), and *Theatrum chemicum* vol. 6 (1659). Note the source is a *different manuscript* — I have not seen evidence that Add MS 5245 itself contains these words; the pairing of text and plates is the editorial tradition, worth verifying against the BL catalogue.

> **The practise of Mary the Prophetess in the Alchymicall Art.**
>
> Aros the Philosopher had a meeting with Mary the Prophetess the Sister of Moyses, and approaching to her, he paid her respect and said unto her. O Prophetess, I have truly heard many say of you that you whiten the Stone in one day.
>
> And Mary said, Yea, Aros, even in a part of one day.
>
> Aros said: O Lady Mary, when will the Work be which you affirm? How shall we whiten and afterwards add blackness?
>
> Mary said; O Aros, oftentimes Nations have dyd about this part. Know you not, O Aros; that there is a water or a thing which whitens Hendragem?
>
> Then Aros answering said to her. O Lady it is so as you say, but in a long time.
>
> Mary answered, Hermes in all his Books has said that the Philosophers whiten the Stone in one hour of the day.
>
> Aros said to her, Oh how excellent is that?
>
> Mary said, it is most excellent to him that is ignorant of it.
>
> Aros said, if men have all the four Elements, he [Hermes] said that their fumes might be compleated, and complexioned, and coagulated, and retained in one day, untill they doe fullfill the consequence (i.e. attain the end).
>
> Mary said, O Aros, by God, if thy senses or understanding were not solid, you should not hear these words from me, untill the Lord should fill my Heart with the grace of his divine Will. Nevertheless take the Allum of Spain, the white gumm and the red gumm, which is the Kibric of the Philosophers, and their Sol and the greater Tincture, and marry Gumm with Gumm togeather with a true Matrimony. Mary said, make them like a running Water, and vitrify this water which has been laboured or wrought upon for one day, out of the two Lubechs, upon the fixed body, and liquefy them by the secret of Nature in the Vessel of Philosophy. Did you understand us?
>
> Yes Lady.
>
> Mary said, Keep the fume and take care that none of it fly away. And let your measure be with a gentle fire such as is the Measure of the heat of the Sun in the Month of June or July, and stay by your Vessel and behold it with care how it grows black, grows red, and grows white in less than three hours of the day, and the fume will penetrate the body, and the Spirit will be bound up, and they will be like milk, incerating, and liquefying and penetrating: and that is the secret.
>
> Aros said I do not say that this will be allways.
>
> Mary said unto him: Aros, and this is more wonderfull concerning this, that it was not among the Ancients, nor did it come to him by curing, or by the Medicinall Art and that is take the white, clear and honoured Herb growing on the Hillocks, and pound it fresh as it is in its Hour, and that is the true Body not flying from the Fire.
>
> And Aros said it is the Stone of Truth?
>
> And Mary said yes. But yet men know not this regimen (rule or way of working) with the speediness thereof.
>
> Aros said, and what afterwards.
>
> Mary said, vitrify upon it Kibric or Zibeic and there are the two fumes comprehending the two Lights, and project upon that the complement of the Tinctures of the Spirits, and the weights of Truth, and pound it all, and put it to the Fire, and you shall see wonderfull things from them. The whole goverment consists in the temper of the Fire, O how strange it is, how it will be moved from one colour to another, in less than an hour of the Day, untill it arrive at the mark of redness and whiteness, and cast away the Fire and permit it to cool, and open it and you will find the clear pearly Body to be of the Colour of the Poppy of the Wood mixt with whiteness and that is it which is incerating, liquefying and penetrating, and one golden piece thereof, the weight of a small golden Coin, falleth upon a thousand thousand and two hundred thousand. That is the hidden secret.
>
> Then Aros fell down upon his face.
>
> And Mary said to him, Lift up your head Aros: because I will shorten for you the thing, as that clear body which is thrown upon the Hillocks, and is not obtained by putrefaction or motion. Take and pound it with Gumm Elsaron, and with the two fumes because the Body comprehending or retaining them is Gumm Elsaron and grind it all. Therefore approach because it all melts. If you project its wife upon it, it will be as a distilling Water, and when the Aire shall strike it, it will be congealed and be one body, and make projection of it, and you will behold Wonders: O Aros that is the hidden secret of Scholia; and know that the said two fumes are the Root of this Art, and they are the white Kibric and the humid calx, but the fixed Body is of the Heart of Saturn comprehending the Tincture, and the Fields of Wisdom or of Scholia. And the Philosophers have named it by many and all names, and received or gathered from the Hillocks it is a clear white Body, and these are the medicine of this Art, part is procured and part is found upon the Hillocks; and know Aros that the wise men have not called it the Fields of Wisdom, or of Scholia, unless because Scholia will not be compleated but by it; and in the Scholia there are nothing but wonderfull things. For there also enters into them the four Stones, and its true regimen is as I have said. And that is first Scoyare, Ade, and Zethet; by that make your Allegory as Hermes has done in his Books Scoyas, and the Philosophers have allways made the regimen longer, and have resembled the work to every thing which ought not to make the work, and they make the Magistery to be in one year, and this but onely for hiding it from the ignorant people, untill it be confirmed in their Hearts and their senses (till they believe the Art),: because the Art will not be compleated except only in Gold; because it is the great secret of God: and they who hear of our secrets doe not verify them (nor believe them to be true), by reason of their ignorance. Did you understand Aros?
>
> Aros said yes. But tell me about that vessel, without which the Work will not be accomplisht.
>
> Mary said that the Vessel of Hermes which the Stoicks have concealed, and it is not a Necromanticall Vessel, but it is the Measure of your Fire.
>
> Aros said: O Lady you have obeyed (been a scholar) in the Society of Scoyari: O Prophetess, have you found among the Secrets of the Philosophers, which they have set down in their Books, that any one can make the Art out of one Body?
>
> And Mary said: Yes: which Hermes has not taught, because the Root of Scholia is a body which cannot be made smooth or plain and incurable (unconquerable) and it is a poyson mortifying all bodies, and it pulverises them and coagulates Mercury with its odour. And shee said, I swear to you by the eternall God, that that poison when it is dissolved untill it is made a subtile Water, I care not by what dissolution it is done, does coagulate Mercury into Luna with the strength of Truth, and it falls into the Throne of Jupiter, and forms him into Luna. And the Science is in all Bodys, but the Stoicks, by reason of the shortness of their lives, and the Length of the Work, have concealed this; and they found out these tinging Elements, and they increased them, and all the Philosophers teach them, except the Vessel of Hermes, because it is divine, and of the Wisdom of the Lord hidden from Nations: and they who are ignorant of it, know not the Regimen of Truth, because of their Ignorance of the Vessel of Hermes.
>
> Mary briefly soundeth forth
> Strange things like Thunder round the Earth.
> She with two Gumms makes the Bottome stay
> What else would fly away.
> Three things if you three hours attend
> Are chained together in the End.
> Mary the Light of dew, and Art has got
> In three hours to tye the Knot.
> Pluto's daughter, it is she
> Who bindeth Loves confederacy
> Joyned with three seeds she does aspire
> To be exalted in the Fire.

## Open questions

- ~~What the second part was meant to say here is absent; the BL copy (Add MS 5245) would be the place to compare.~~ Partly resolved: the BL Second Book is picture-only too (five plates, two of them absent from the Warburg copy), and the tradition supplies the Mary the Prophetess dialogue as its text — but whether Add MS 5245 itself contains that text, or the pairing is purely editorial, I could not verify.
- The label on the wrung cloth in Plate 1 (`Volatile [?]`) and the flask-label `Vegetatica`/`Vegetativa` in Plate 4 are the two readings I am least sure of.
- The plates never name the matter of the *fodina*; the urine reading rests on the imagery of Plate 2, not on the captions.
- "The Vessel of Hermes … is not a Necromanticall Vessel, but it is the Measure of your Fire" — the vessel *is* the fire-regimen. Sits directly against the sealed-vessel and temperature/gestation threads.
