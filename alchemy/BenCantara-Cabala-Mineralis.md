# Cabala mineralis — Rabbi Simeon ben Cantara

**File:** `BenCantara-Cabala-Mineralis.pdf` (Warburg Institute scan, classmark FGH 6480, Innes Collection)
**Source:** https://wdl.warburg.sas.ac.uk/object-wdl-wco-aaez
**Companion contents:** full Latin transcription of every caption and label in the manuscript, with English translation.

## How this was made, and what to trust

The transcription below is my own reading of the 17th-century hand from the Warburg scans (PDF pages 7–17; the rest of the file is covers, endpapers, and blanks). The hand is unusually clean and most readings are secure. Where a reading is uncertain it is marked `[?]`; where the scribe's spelling is non-classical I have kept it as written and noted the standard form in brackets. The translations are working translations, not scholarly ones — check them against the Latin, which is short enough to read line by line.

The manuscript uses astrological sigils, rendered here as: ☉ = Sol (gold), ☽ = Luna (silver). The scribe adds case endings as superscripts (☉is = Solis, ☽ae = Lunae).

**A note on this copy:** the better-known copy of the *Cabala mineralis* is British Library Add MS 5245, which carries captions throughout both parts. In this Warburg copy the second part is a title spread and figure drawings only — the ruled caption boxes and a speech-scroll are left empty, and several caption boxes in part one are also blank. It looks like an unfinished copy, but that is my inference from the blanks, not something the catalogue record states.

## Plate 1 — the mine of our Mercury (PDF p. 8)

*Scene:* under a tree, a boy in red stoops to the ground holding tongs, an hourglass on his head labelled **Mercury**; the ground is labelled **Fodina** (the mine). A flowering plant with blue blossoms stands in a pot over a small fire, eagles perched on it, labelled **ponticitas**. A second figure wrings a cloth (label small and blurred: `Volatile [?]`) over a shallow pan held by a disembodied hand; below, a flask on a fire surrounded by a flock of eagles, and at far right a small sealed vessel over fire.

**Latin (caption box):**

> Fodina Mercurii nostri sal petra nostrum non vulgi.
> Ponticitas vitriolum nostrum non vulgi Acuatio.
> Armoniacum nostrum non vulgi.

**English:**

> The mine of our Mercury; our saltpetre, not the common sort.
> Ponticity [sharpness]: our vitriol, not the common — the sharpening.
> Our [sal] ammoniac, not the common.

The point of the triple "non vulgi" is that the named substances (saltpetre, vitriol, sal ammoniac) are cover-names: the work does not use the apothecary's materials but "ours," drawn from the *fodina*.

## Plate 2 — the seed of Mercury (PDF p. 10)

*Scene, left:* a green winged dragon lies on a dish under raining clouds; labels **Mercurii Germen** and, beneath the dish, **Hermaphroditum**. *Centre:* a cucurbit with still-head over fire, and inside it a child, shown urinating, labelled **Mercurius Homogeneus**. *Right:* a long-necked flask over fire; inside, a caduceus rises from a yellow deposit, labelled **Mercurius vivus**.

**Latin (left caption box):**

> Mercurii Germen, Aquae vivae, volatilis, partes, novem, Fixae pars una Mercurii, Homogenitas Aqua viva Acuata.

**English:**

> The Seed of Mercury: nine parts of the volatile living water, one part of the fixed [part] of Mercury. Homogeneity: the living water sharpened.

**Latin (right caption box):**

> Mercurius noster Aqua viva pontica.

**English:**

> Our Mercury: the pontic [sharp] living water.

This is the plate behind the common reading (argued by Adam McLean among others) that the manuscript's raw matter is urine: the child urinating inside the vessel labelled "homogeneous Mercury" is hard to read any other way, though the text itself never says the word — it speaks only of *aqua viva*.

## Plate 3 — amalgamation and philosophic calcination (PDF p. 11)

*Scene:* two flasks over fire, each holding a winged cherub standing behind a dark spongy mass, eagles flying at both sides. In the left flask the cherub holds a crescent moon; in the right, a sun, with labels **Calcinatio Sophica** and **Solis** around the flask.

**Latin (left caption box):**

> Duae, ad plus Tres partes, Mercurii nostri, Liguant [= ligant] unam partem ☽ae vel ☉is, vulgi subtilitati, et Corpus unum, fiunt, Spongiosum, inseparabile, Quod vocatur ☽ nostra, vel ☉ noster, non vulgi
> ☽. per Mercurium Aquilarum Quinque
> ☉. per Mercurium Aquilarum Septem.

**English:**

> Two, at most three parts of our Mercury bind one part of common Silver or Gold [first] subtilised, and they become one body, spongy, inseparable, which is called our Silver or our Gold, not the common.
> Silver: by the Mercury of five eagles.
> Gold: by the Mercury of seven eagles.

("Eagles" is the standard cover-term for repeated sublimations — the eagle-flocks drawn around the vessels throughout the manuscript are counting sublimations, not decorating.)

The right caption box is ruled but empty.

## Plate 4 — putrefaction and germination (PDF p. 12)

*Scene, left:* flask over fire, contents jet black, cherub standing in it, three eagles; labels **putrefactio Sophica**. *Right:* flask over fire whose dark surface shows red glints and rising smoke; labels **Germinatio** and **Vegetatica** [? possibly *Vegetativa*]. A ribbon-scroll connects the two flasks.

**Latin (left caption box):**

> ☉. noster, de Calcinatione, nostra, irrigatam [sic; sense requires *irrigatus*] novi Mercurii, partibus duabus in pondere, ☉is vulgi fit corpus unum nigerrimum non Spongiosum.

**English:**

> Our Gold, from our calcination, irrigated with new Mercury — two parts by weight against the common gold — becomes one utterly black body, not spongy.

**Latin (right caption box):**

> ☉ de nigredine Colore Mutatô viridascens Germen, suum pandit in vegetationem.

**English:**

> The Gold, its colour changed from blackness, growing green, unfolds its seed into vegetation.

## Plate 5 — the White Sulphur and the wet volatilisation (PDF p. 13)

*Scene, left:* flask over fire half-filled with white scaly grains, labelled **Sulphur Album**. *Right:* flask over fire, dark iridescent surface with coloured vapours above, labels **Liquidum** and (vertically) **volatilisatio per**. Ribbon-scroll between them.

**Latin (left caption box):**

> ☉ noster mutatô Colore virid[i] [?], Transit in Sulphur Album, Quod est Corpus Animatum Animâ inCorruptibili.

**English:**

> Our Gold, its green colour changed, passes into the White Sulphur, which is a body animated by an incorruptible soul.

**Latin (right caption box):**

> ☉. Hic noster albus, sine Manuum impositione ulla, nec vasis, Apertione. iterum liquescit, Calore Continuatô cum variis vaporibus. ad. cauda pavonis. similitudinem, Tunc ☉ noster. Albus. volatilisatur per Liquidum.

**English:**

> This our white Gold — without any laying-on of hands, and without opening the vessel — liquefies again under continued heat, with various vapours in the likeness of the peacock's tail; then our white Gold is volatilised through the liquid [the wet way].

Note the operative claim: from here the work proceeds *sine manuum impositione* — sealed, untouched, driven by heat alone.

## Plate 6 — the dry volatilisation and the Red Sulphur (PDF p. 14)

*Scene, left:* flask over fire, contents drying from brown to red, labels (vertically) **volatilisatio per** and **siccum**. *Right:* flask over fire filled with deep-red grains, labelled **Sulphur Rubeum**. Ribbon-scroll between them.

**Latin (left caption box):**

> Continuatô Calore ☉ noster volatilis, jam factus per Liquidum desiccatur et fit Corpus Lucens, Quasi Aurora Consurgens, Haec est volatilisatio per siccum.

**English:**

> With continued heat our volatile Gold, now made [volatile] through the liquid, is dried and becomes a shining body, like the rising dawn. This is volatilisation by the dry [way].

**Latin (right caption box):**

> Hac Aurora paulatim Evanescente, consurgit ☉ noster in Granula pulchrima [= pulcherrima], et Rubicundissima, Que [= Quae] sunt Sulphur nostrum Rubeum a Sophis, Tam desideratum, Quod tamen non est finis Laborum.

**English:**

> As this dawn gradually fades, our Gold rises up into little grains, most beautiful and deepest red, which are our Red Sulphur so longed for by the sages — which nevertheless is not the end of the labours.

## Plate 7 — imbibition and the Stone (PDF p. 15)

*Scene, left:* flask over fire; inside, a cherub stands in a landscape of coloured grains with smoke and small flames, labels around the flask: **fermentatio, alias Imbibitio Lapidis**. *Right:* flask (no fire beneath) half-filled with red grains, labelled **Lapis Sophorum**, and beneath it **Medecina tertii ordinis**. Ribbon-scroll between them.

**Latin (left caption box):**

> Sulphur hoc rubeum, imbibitur novi Mercurii Quinario, sui corporis pondere, paulatim et paulatim, per septem diversas Imbibitiones, et continuatô Calore Rotatâ rotâ Naturae, mense unô, Transeunt putrefactio et jam dicta Regimina.

**English:**

> This red Sulphur is imbibed with a fivefold [measure] of new Mercury, by the weight of its own body, little by little, through seven distinct imbibitions; and with continued heat, the wheel of Nature having been turned, in one month the putrefaction and the regimens already described pass by [again].

**Latin (right caption box):**

> Finito Mense, surgit Rex, omnipotens, seu Lapis noster, perfecta Medecina, tertii ordinis, potens in sui projectione, omnia Metalla transmutare.
> Deo. sit Aeterna Laus et Gratiarum actio incessabilis Amen.

**English:**

> The month being finished, there arises the almighty King, or our Stone, the perfect Medicine of the third order, powerful in its projection to transmute all metals.
> To God be eternal praise and unceasing thanksgiving. Amen.

## The Second part of this Booke (PDF pp. 16–17)

The part-title (PDF p. 16) is in **English**, not Latin — the only English in the manuscript:

> The Second part of this Booke.

*Scene:* a full-spread landscape. An old bearded man (Saturn-type) leaps barefoot above the ground, holding a pair of compasses in one hand and a branch of red berries in the other, a latticed sphere hanging below; above him a face-in-sun sheds gold coin-like stars. To the right: a white dog chasing a hare up a hill, a palm tree, a red ox, and a black swan sitting on a nest.

PDF p. 17 carries four figure drawings (a man lying on a rolled mat with drops falling from his body and an empty speech-scroll at his mouth; an old man reaching upward; a flying winged figure in blue; a standing youth) — **no text**; the scroll and the following ruled spreads are blank.

## Open questions

- What the second part was meant to say here is absent; the BL copy (Add MS 5245) would be the place to compare.
- The label on the wrung cloth in Plate 1 (`Volatile [?]`) and the flask-label `Vegetatica`/`Vegetativa` in Plate 4 are the two readings I am least sure of.
- The plates never name the matter of the *fodina*; the urine reading rests on the imagery of Plate 2, not on the captions.
